My Story

My journey into education wasn't planned — it was called. I grew up watching teachers shape lives with very little resources, and I knew early on that I wanted to be part of that mission. But I also knew the system needed changing.

After years of working in the classroom as a teacher and curriculum developer, I took the bold step of founding my own school. It wasn't easy — there were late nights, financial pressures, and moments of deep doubt. But every time a student's eye lit up with understanding, it confirmed that this path was worth it.

"Education isn't about filling a bucket — it's about lighting a fire. My job is to make sure every child I touch leaves burning with potential."

Why I Started Life in Bytes

I noticed a gap. While I was navigating the complex world of school ownership, parenting, and educational technology, there were very few authentic voices speaking to the African educator's experience. Most content out there was either too theoretical or too foreign to be applicable.

So I started writing. Not from a place of having all the answers, but from the trenches — sharing real experiences, tested strategies, and honest reflections. Life in Bytes was born from that desire to document and share.

What I Write About

This blog sits at the intersection of five core areas that I'm deeply passionate about:

  • Education — Teaching methodologies, curriculum innovation, and student development
  • Tech & AI — How emerging technologies can transform African classrooms and careers
  • School Leadership — The nuts and bolts of running a sustainable, thriving school
  • Parenting — Raising intentional, digitally wise children in a complex world
  • Personal Growth & Faith — The spiritual and personal disciplines that sustain purpose-driven people

My goal is simple: equip you to educate better, lead smarter, parent with intention, and live with purpose.

Beyond the Blog

Outside of writing, I actively work on educational initiatives, conduct training workshops for school leaders, and am currently completing a book on building schools in the digital age. I also mentor young men, because I believe the future is shaped by what we pour into the next generation today.

When I'm not immersed in work, I'm spending quality time with my family — my greatest classroom and most honest audience.
